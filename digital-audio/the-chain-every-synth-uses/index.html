The chain every synth uses

Oscillator, filter, amp — three parts you already know, wired in a row, are what makes a synth bass sound like an instrument.

Before you start

Sawtooth wave
a wave that climbs steadily then drops straight back down, like the teeth of a saw. It is packed with harmonics, which makes it bright and buzzy and a good starting point for a synth.
Harmonics
the quieter extra frequencies riding above the note you actually name. They sit at whole-number multiples of it, so a 100 Hz note has harmonics at 200 Hz, 300 Hz, 400 Hz and on up.
Cutoff
the frequency where a filter starts removing things. Set the cutoff low and only the deepest parts survive; raise it and the brightness comes back.
Envelope
the shape of a sound’s loudness from start to finish. A pluck jumps to full volume and dies away; a bowed note swells in and holds.
ADSR = attack, decay, sustain, release
the four stages of an envelope. How fast it arrives, the drop to a steady level, the level it holds while you keep the note down, and how long it takes to fade once you let go.

Where you meet this every day

Put on almost any dance track from the last forty years and listen underneath the drums. That round, rubbery bass line isn’t a bass guitar. It’s a synth doing the same three jobs in the same order, every time.

Three parts in a row

The order is oscillator → filter → amp.

The oscillator is the part that makes the wobble in the first place — to oscillate is literally the scientist’s word for wobbling back and forth — the source everything else only shapes. Give it a sawtooth and it starts bright and buzzy, packed with harmonics, too bright to be musical on its own.

The filter cuts the top off. Drop the cutoff and the harmonics disappear from the top down, which is what turns a buzz into something round enough to sit under a track.

The amp — short for amplifier — controls loudness. On its own it would just switch the sound on and off, which is why an envelope drives it: attack, decay, sustain, release decide how the note arrives and how it leaves.

Only the oscillator is new. Harmonics, cutoff and envelopes you have already met separately. Wired in a row, they stop being ideas about sound and become an instrument you can play.
