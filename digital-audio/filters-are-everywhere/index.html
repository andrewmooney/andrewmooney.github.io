Filters are everywhere

Why music through a wall is all bass — and how one control on a synth does the same thing on purpose.

Before you start

Frequency
how fast something wobbles. Your ear hears frequency as pitch — the faster the wobble, the higher the note.
Hz = hertz
cycles per second — how many complete wobbles fit into one second. 440 Hz means 440 every second, which is the A that an orchestra tunes to.
Harmonics
the quieter extra frequencies riding above the note you actually name. They sit at whole-number multiples of it, so a 100 Hz note has harmonics at 200 Hz, 300 Hz, 400 Hz and on up.
Sawtooth wave
a wave that climbs steadily then drops straight back down, like the teeth of a saw. It is packed with harmonics, which makes it bright and buzzy and a good starting point for a synth.

Where you meet this every day

Stand outside a party and you hear the thump of the bass, but almost none of the singing or the cymbals. The wall isn’t turning the music down evenly. Low frequencies pass through it far more easily than high ones, so what reaches you is the bottom of the sound with the top stripped off. The same thing happens to voices through a closed door, or to a song playing in the car next to you.

Cutting a band out of the sound

A filter removes part of the frequency range and leaves the rest. The wall is a low-pass filter: lows pass, highs don’t. The control that matters is the cutoff — the frequency where the filter starts removing things. Set the cutoff low and only the deepest parts survive; raise it and more of the sound comes back.

Filters usually have a second control, resonance, which boosts the frequencies sitting right at the cutoff instead of removing them. A little resonance makes the cutoff audible as a ringing edge, so you can hear exactly where the filter is working.

Try it

Open synth-lab.html#m03. Play a sawtooth and sweep the cutoff down. Because a sawtooth is a stack of harmonics, you hear them vanish from the top down — bright, then hollow, then almost a pure tone. Then add resonance and sweep again.
