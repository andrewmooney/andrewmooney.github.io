Sound is just numbers

Hold music sounds like it is coming from inside a biscuit tin — and the two decisions behind that are the same two behind every recording you own.

Before you start

Frequency
how fast something wobbles. Your ear hears frequency as pitch — the faster the wobble, the higher the note.
Hz = hertz
cycles per second — how many complete wobbles fit into one second. 440 Hz means 440 every second, which is the A that an orchestra tunes to.
Harmonics
the quieter extra frequencies riding above the note you actually name. They sit at whole-number multiples of it, so a 100 Hz note has harmonics at 200 Hz, 300 Hz, 400 Hz and on up.
Amplitude
how far each wobble swings from the resting line. Your ear hears amplitude as loudness — bigger swings, louder sound.

Where you meet this every day

You ring the council and they put you on hold. The song down the line is one you know, but it arrives thin and grey — the cymbals have gone, and the singer sounds like she is calling from inside a biscuit tin. The recording at the other end is perfectly good. What you are hearing is a phone line measuring that music far too rarely, and far too roughly.

Two decisions turn a wave into a list

A microphone turns air being pushed into a smooth electrical wave, and a chip immediately chops that wave into numbers. Only two decisions govern the chopping, and between them they settle everything about how the recording comes back.

The first is how often to measure — the sample rate. Streamed music is measured 44,100 times a second; a phone call manages 8,000. Measure that rarely and the fastest wiggles fall between the measurements, so the harmonics that make a voice recognisable never reach the list at all. That is the biscuit tin.

The second is how precisely each measurement gets written down — the bit depth. A bit is one yes-or-no, and more of them stacked together write down bigger, more exact numbers. With plenty of bits, a measurement can sit almost exactly on the wave. With only a few, every measurement has to round to the nearest rung of a coarse ladder, and all that rounding error piles up as a gritty hiss underneath the music.

Set both generously and the list of numbers traces the original closely enough that your ear cannot tell the two apart. Set either meanly and you get hold music.

Try it

Open synth-lab.html#m07 and hold a steady tone. Drag the sample rate slider down and the smooth curve breaks into a staircase; drag the bit depth down and those steps grow taller and coarser. Keep both moving until what you hear is unmistakably a phone call.
