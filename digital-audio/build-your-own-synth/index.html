Before you start

Oscillator
the part that makes the wobble in the first place. It is the source of the sound — everything else in a synth only shapes what the oscillator produces.
Filter
something that removes part of the frequency range and leaves the rest. A wall is a filter — the bass gets through and the cymbals do not.
Cutoff
the frequency where a filter starts removing things. Set the cutoff low and only the deepest parts survive; raise it and the brightness comes back.
LFO = low frequency oscillator
an oscillator running far too slowly to hear as a note. You never hear the LFO itself, only what it does to the sound it is steering.
Envelope
the shape of a sound’s loudness from start to finish. A pluck jumps to full volume and dies away; a bowed note swells in and holds.

Where you meet this every day

There is a patch in the lab called Ocean Wind. It sounds like a storm coming in off the water — gusts rising, dying away, rising again. It is made of three things: a noise source — every frequency at once, at random, which you hear as hiss — a filter, and a slow LFO wobbling the filter up and down. No recording of wind is involved. The howl you hear is hiss with its brightness being swept back and forth, and your ears fill in the weather.

The arrangement is the instrument

The chain every synth uses gave you an order to follow: source, then shaping, then out. The Patch Bay takes the order away and hands you the cables instead.

The important part is that there are two kinds of cable and they look the same. A signal cable carries sound you could hear. A control cable carries a number that moves something — the LFO in Ocean Wind never makes a sound of its own, it just tells the filter where to sit, moment by moment. Same cable, same sockets. That is why one set of blocks can be a storm, a bass line or a doorbell: you did not change the blocks, you changed what is plugged into what.

Try it

Open patch-bay.html#preset=basic-chain — the same three-part chain, preloaded and playable. Play a few keys, then unplug one cable at a time and listen for exactly what each break removes. Then load patch-bay.html#preset=ocean-wind and follow the three cables until you can say which one is making the gusts. The MIDI In block — musical instrument digital interface, the language keyboards use to send notes — is what lets your keys trigger the patch.
